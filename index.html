What is the VP OSPO?

A connector, facilitator, and liaison

The Vice President, Open Source Program Office (VP OSPO) is the ASF officer responsible for being the Foundation's primary point of contact with organizations outside the ASF that consume, contribute to, fund, regulate, or collaborate with Apache projects. This includes software vendors and companies (typically via their OSPOs), public sector OSPOs, and other open source foundations.

The VP OSPO acts as a connector: routing external interest to the right people inside the Foundation, helping prospective projects find their way to the ASF, and helping existing Apache projects grow healthy, diverse ecosystems. The role is a facilitator and liaison. It does not take technical, governance, or acceptance decisions away from the bodies that already own them.

The role at a glance

  • Single, well-known entry point to the Foundation
  • Routes external interest to the right PMCs and officers
  • Helps prospective projects find their way to the ASF
  • Supports PMCs that ask for help growing their ecosystems
  • Never takes decisions away from the bodies that own them

Why an OSPO?

Background & rationale

The number of organizations that want to engage with the Foundation keeps growing, and so does the variety of reasons for engaging.

Today these conversations reach the Foundation through many different doors—individual committers, PMCs, officers, board members—which makes it hard for external parties to know whom to talk to, and hard for the Foundation to respond consistently.

The VP OSPO provides a single, well-known entry point, while preserving the ASF's decentralized, community-led model.

  • Companies & Contributors

    Companies want to contribute to projects, donate code, or understand how to participate under the Apache Way.

  • Corporate OSPOs

    Corporate OSPOs want a clear counterpart to discuss contribution policies, security, and long-term sustainability.

  • Public Sector Organizations

    Public sector organizations are adopting open source at scale and increasingly engage on regulation, procurement, digital sovereignty, and software supply-chain security.

  • Peer Open Source Foundations

    Other open source foundations collaborate with the ASF on shared standards, cross-project integrations, policy positions, and joint initiatives.

Security Vulnerability Report

Responsible Point of Contact
ASF Security Team

Reporting a security vulnerability in an Apache project? Always follow the official ASF coordinated vulnerability disclosure process directly.

What happens next

The ASF Security Team owns vulnerability handling directly. VP OSPO coordinates with the Security Team only on broader external supply-chain and regulatory security topics.

Legal, Licensing & Trademarks

Responsible Point of Contact
VP Legal Affairs & VP Brand Management

Specific legal, licensing, CLA/CCLA, or Apache trademark and naming inquiries are governed by the Foundation's dedicated officers.

What happens next

VP Legal Affairs owns legal and licensing matters; VP Brand Management owns trademark and naming questions. VP OSPO routes external questions to them rather than duplicating their authority.
