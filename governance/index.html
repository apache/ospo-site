How the Role Operates

Governance, Principles & Accountability

The VP OSPO is a facilitator and liaison rooted in the Apache Way—grounded in vendor neutrality, PMC autonomy, openness, and clear boundaries with existing ASF officers.

Guiding principles

How the VP OSPO operates

01

Vendor neutrality

The VP OSPO represents the Foundation, not any employer, vendor, or customer. All organizations are treated equally regardless of size, sponsorship level, or commercial relationship with any officer.

02

PMC autonomy

PMCs own the technical direction and community decisions of their projects. The VP OSPO can make introductions and share information, but never commits a project to anything or speaks on its behalf without the PMC's agreement.

03

Facilitate, don't duplicate

Existing officers and committees keep their responsibilities: Legal Affairs for legal questions, Brand Management for trademarks, Fundraising for sponsorship, Security for vulnerability handling, Marketing & Publicity for press. The VP OSPO routes and coordinates.

04

Openness

Consistent with the Apache Way, discussions are held on public mailing lists whenever possible. Private channels are used only when confidentiality is genuinely required (for example, early-stage discussions with an organization or sensitive policy matters), and outcomes are shared publicly once appropriate.

05

Conflict of interest

The VP OSPO discloses any affiliation that could create a conflict of interest and recuses from discussions where appropriate.

Clear boundaries

What the role is not

×

Not a sales or business-development function

Not a sales or business-development function on behalf of any company.

×

Not preferential access

Not a channel for preferential access to projects, roadmaps, or decision-makers.

×

Not a decision-making authority

Not a decision-making authority over project acceptance, project governance, or technical choices.

×

Not a replacement for Fundraising

Not a replacement for Fundraising: sponsorship conversations are handed over to VP Fundraising.

Accountability

Reporting & accountability

  • The VP OSPO reports to the President and contributes to the President's report to the Board.
  • Reports cover: notable external engagements, prospective projects introduced to the ASF, cross-foundation initiatives, public sector engagements, and requests from PMCs.
  • The role is appointed according to the ASF's usual process for officers, and is reviewed periodically like other officer positions.

Outcomes

Measures of success

1

Clear, consistent entry point

External organizations know how to reach the ASF and receive a timely, consistent response.

2

Better-prepared incoming projects

Prospective projects reach the ASF better prepared and with a clearer understanding of the Apache Way.

3

Valuable PMC introductions

PMCs report useful introductions and new contributors or adopters resulting from OSPO activity.

4

Constructive public & cross-foundation voice

The ASF is a recognized and constructive participant in public sector and cross-foundation discussions about open source.

5

Trusted vendor neutrality

No complaints of preferential treatment or vendor bias.

The Open Source Program Office (OSPO) makes it easy for organizations worldwide to engage with The Apache Software Foundation in a manner consistent with the Apache Way and vendor neutrality.
