## Summary

The Vice President, Open Source Program Office (VP OSPO) is the ASF officer responsible for being the Foundation's primary point of contact with organizations outside the ASF that consume, contribute to, fund, regulate, or collaborate with Apache projects. This includes software vendors and companies (typically via their OSPOs), public sector OSPOs, and other open source foundations.

The VP OSPO acts as a connector: routing external interest to the right people inside the Foundation, helping prospective projects find their way to the ASF, and helping existing Apache projects grow healthy, diverse ecosystems. The role is a facilitator and liaison. It does not take technical, governance, or acceptance decisions away from the bodies that already own them.

## Background and Rationale

The ASF hosts hundreds of projects relied upon by companies and governments worldwide. The number of organizations that want to engage with the Foundation keeps growing, and so does the variety of reasons for engaging:

- Companies want to contribute to projects, donate code, or understand how to participate under the Apache Way.
- Corporate OSPOs want a clear counterpart to discuss contribution policies, security, and long-term sustainability.
- Public sector organizations are adopting open source at scale and increasingly engage on regulation, procurement, digital sovereignty, and software supply-chain security.
- Other open source foundations collaborate with the ASF on shared standards, cross-project integrations, policy positions, and joint initiatives.

Today these conversations reach the Foundation through many different doors (individual committers, PMCs, officers, board members), which makes it hard for external parties to know whom to talk to, and hard for the Foundation to respond consistently. The VP OSPO provides a single, well-known entry point, while preserving the ASF's decentralized, community-led model.

## Mission

> **Make it easy for the world to engage with the Apache Software Foundation, and help Apache projects and communities thrive through those relationships, in a way that is consistent with the Apache Way and the Foundation's vendor neutrality.**

## Responsibilities

### Primary contact for external organizations

#### Vendors and companies

- Serve as the first point of contact for companies that want to engage with the ASF or with specific Apache projects.
- Explain how to participate effectively: contributing as individuals, merit-based governance, licensing and CLAs/CCLAs, trademark usage, and the separation between employers and project roles.
- Connect companies with the appropriate PMCs and officers (Brand Management, Legal Affairs, Fundraising, Security, etc.), rather than acting on their behalf.
- Engage with corporate OSPOs on topics such as contribution policies, sustainability, and upstream-first practices.

#### Public sector

- Act as the ASF's liaison to governments, public administrations, regulators, and public research institutions.
- Help public sector organizations understand how they can use, contribute to, and support Apache projects.
- Coordinate, together with the relevant officers (Public Affairs, Legal Affairs, Security, Privacy, the President and the Board), the ASF's engagement on public policy topics affecting open source, such as cybersecurity regulation, procurement, and digital sovereignty initiatives.
- Make sure the ASF speaks with one consistent voice in these discussions.

#### Other open source foundations and communities

- Maintain working relationships with peer foundations and open source organizations.
- Identify opportunities for collaboration: shared tooling, interoperability between projects, joint events, common positions on policy, and shared best practices.
- Help resolve questions where projects span or depend on multiple foundations.

#### Norms, standards, and legal discussions

- Involved alongside other ASF Vice Presidents in norms, standards, and legal discussions affecting the Foundation and its communities.

### Helping new projects come to the ASF

- Be a visible entry point for organizations and communities considering bringing a project to the ASF.
- Explain what the ASF offers and expects, and help them assess whether the ASF is the right home.
- Connect prospective projects with the ASF, helping them join the Foundation and connecting with relevant existing PMCs.
- Help connect the different teams involved in onboarding (Legal Affairs, Brand Management, Infrastructure, Security) so prospective projects have a smooth experience.
- The decision to accept a project remains entirely with the Board. The VP OSPO facilitates and recommends, but does not decide or vote on acceptance by virtue of the role.

### Helping Apache projects expand their ecosystems

- Help PMCs that ask for it to find new contributors, adopters, and collaborators, including companies, public institutions, and research organizations.
- Highlight integration opportunities between Apache projects, and between Apache projects and projects in other foundations.
- Share adoption stories, needs, and feedback from external organizations with the relevant PMCs.
- Work with Marketing & Publicity and Conferences to give visibility to projects and their ecosystems.
- Encourage diversity of contributors and employers within project communities, which is key to project resilience.

## Guiding Principles

**Vendor neutrality.** The VP OSPO represents the Foundation, not any employer, vendor, or customer. All organizations are treated equally regardless of size, sponsorship level, or commercial relationship with any officer.

**PMC autonomy.** PMCs own the technical direction and community decisions of their projects. The VP OSPO can make introductions and share information, but never commits a project to anything or speaks on its behalf without the PMC's agreement.

**Facilitate, don't duplicate.** Existing officers and committees keep their responsibilities: Legal Affairs for legal questions, Brand Management for trademarks, Fundraising for sponsorship, Security for vulnerability handling, Marketing & Publicity for press. The VP OSPO routes and coordinates.

**Openness.** Consistent with the Apache Way, discussions are held on public mailing lists whenever possible. Private channels are used only when confidentiality is genuinely required (for example, early-stage discussions with an organization or sensitive policy matters), and outcomes are shared publicly once appropriate.

**Conflict of interest.** The VP OSPO discloses any affiliation that could create a conflict of interest and recuses from discussions where appropriate.

## What the Role Is Not

- Not a sales or business-development function on behalf of any company.
- Not a channel for preferential access to projects, roadmaps, or decision-makers.
- Not a decision-making authority over project acceptance, project governance, or technical choices.
- Not a replacement for Fundraising: sponsorship conversations are handed over to VP Fundraising.

## Working Relationships

| Counterpart | Nature of the relationship |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Board of Directors** | Receives regular reports; consulted on strategic or policy engagements |
| **President** | The VP OSPO reports to the President as an operational officer |
| **Apache Incubator PMC** | Main partner for prospective projects |
| **PMCs** | Partners for ecosystem expansion; engaged on request or with their agreement |
| **VP Legal Affairs** | Legal and licensing questions, policy and regulatory topics |
| **VP Brand Management** | Trademark and naming questions raised by external parties |
| **VP Fundraising** | Hand-off for sponsorship and donation discussions |
| **Security Team** | Supply-chain, vulnerability, and regulatory security topics |
| **VP Marketing & Publicity** | Public communication, announcements, and press |
| **VP Conferences** | Joint events and ecosystem visibility |
| **VP Infrastructure** | Onboarding needs of incoming projects |

## Reporting and Accountability

- The VP OSPO reports to the President and contributes to the President's report to the Board.
- Reports cover: notable external engagements, prospective projects introduced to the ASF, cross-foundation initiatives, public sector engagements, and requests from PMCs.
- The role is appointed according to the ASF's usual process for officers, and is reviewed periodically like other officer positions.

## Measures of Success

- External organizations know how to reach the ASF and receive a timely, consistent response.
- Prospective projects reach the ASF better prepared and with a clearer understanding of the Apache Way.
- PMCs report useful introductions and new contributors or adopters resulting from OSPO activity.
- The ASF is a recognized and constructive participant in public sector and cross-foundation discussions about open source.
- No complaints of preferential treatment or vendor bias.

## Communication Channels

- A public mailing list for general OSPO discussions (`discuss@ospo.apache.org`), open to the community.
- A direct contact address (`contact@ospo.apache.org`) for external organizations and confidential contacts, with outcomes reported publicly when possible.
- This site (with a direct link from `www.apache.org`) describing the role, how to contact the OSPO, and what to expect.
